# The process (v1, a theory to test on the next game)

Status: drafted Nov 7 2026 from Dan's answers, one question at a time, after the review of how Critter Roll was made (`docs/process-review.md`). Dan said before the conversation: "what we stated today is just a theory". It stays a theory until a game has been made with it. Each rule below carries the answer it came from.

## Roles

- **Dan** writes the meaning, the last act and every station; makes the grey boxes, the assets and the design; plays; decides.
- **The AI** reads back and sharpens, builds playable placeholders, measures what can be measured, says what it cannot judge, and asks until it can say back what Dan wants.

## 1. The meaning

A game starts with one sentence: **a truth about people**. The game is the argument, played. Dan writes the first version; the AI reads it back in its own words and proposes sharpenings; Dan decides. Nothing of the game exists before the sentence does.

Critter Roll's sentence was found in the content after the fact. That is the mistake this rule corrects.

## 2. The last act

Second comes **the last act**: what the player does at the end that proves the sentence. Only the act is defined at this point; the opening is not.

The test of a last act: **the player does it unasked**. Nothing tells the player to do it; the road has made them want to. If it would need a prompt, it is the wrong act.

## 3. The road, backwards

From the last act the road is worked **backwards**. The unit is the **station**: one piece of the sentence that the player meets once, with three parts:

- its **meaning**: what it says inside the sentence;
- its **hint**: the first, smaller, quieter encounter with it, placed earlier;
- its **act**: what the player does that carries it. The mechanic is the meaning, not a text or a prop that rhymes with it.

Dan fills the stations, asking the AI questions as he goes. The question asked at each step back ("what must they have done once, smaller?", "what must they want by then?", "what must they have, and have lost?") **depends on the game** and is chosen per game, not fixed by the process.

The road has **a few paths to the same act**. Stations can be met in different orders, so a hint must work in any order it can be met in, and the last act is the same for everyone.

**Hidden things are stations too.** A secret has the same three parts; it is simply off every path and found by staying or looking. It is filled like any other station, never added just to be found.

Scope is not a number of stations. It is whatever the road needs between the first station and the act; the review notes that the opening failed first in Critter Roll, so the first station gets the same care as the last.

## 4. Building a station

Order, for each station once its three parts are written:

1. **Dan makes a grey box**: a rough layout, the intent, and what happens.
2. **The AI fills it out**: builds the act playable, with placeholders for every asset not yet made (art, sound, words where needed). Placeholders are the only thing built to find out; nothing finished is built before its station is filled.
3. **Dan plays it** and corrects.
4. **Once the feel is right, Dan replaces the placeholders** with his assets and design.

Generation exists only for placeholders. Nothing generated ships.

## 5. Handover and play

What must be true before a station reaches Dan, both at once:

- **a bot with real defaults has reached the station and performed its act**; anything that moves was checked over real time against the clock; the words were linted for telling; and the report says plainly what only Dan can judge (feel, tempo, taste, whether the hint is noticed);
- **it runs on Dan's phone**, and his play is the test.

The unit of work is **one station at a time**. Dan plays when he can; what has piled up since his last play is the batch.

## 6. Corrections

When Dan comes back with a correction, the AI **asks questions until it can say back what he wants**, and he confirms, before anything is rebuilt. No rebuilding from one line. (Critter Roll's Arena: four versions in seventy minutes from one-line briefs.)

## 7. Agreement

A game is a whole; every element of a station (layout, what is seen and hidden, colour, sound, words, pacing) must carry the station's intent. An element that says something else, or nothing, is a bug of meaning.

The agreement check is done **by both, at the end of a batch**: after Dan plays, each station is gone through element by element, and whatever disagrees is named. Fixes go into the next batch. The AI can lint only two things on its own: words that tell, and a hint missing before its act.

## 8. Done, and the shelf

A game is finished **when it is shown**: it goes on the shelf as a cartridge and nothing more is added, whatever state it is in. Finishing is a decision, not a condition.

The shelf is the showing. A cartridge carries a number, a name, a year and the record on the device, nothing more. The game speaks for itself once opened.

## What this does not yet say

- How the opening is derived once the road reaches it, and what the first minute must do (Critter Roll: "most players loose interest after a minute").
- What a grey box is concretely (a drawing, a tile map, a list?), and in what form Dan hands it over. This is the engine question, and the engine is not started.
- Whether the sentence can change once stations exist, and what happens to the stations that were built under the old one (in Critter Roll a sharpened ending reversed a correct metaphor).
- How many games this process is for, and whether small games (one station) are the first test of it.
