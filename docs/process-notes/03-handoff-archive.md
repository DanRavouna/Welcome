# Critter Roll: what the record says about the process (creation, iteration, meaning)

Sources: `docs/handoff-archive.md` (read whole), `critter-roll-handoff.md` (read whole), `CLAUDE.md`, and the git history (`git log --all`, Nov 3 to Nov 7 2026; the Nov 3 to Nov 5 work is squashed into one commit, so its order comes from the archive's section order and its internal cross-references). Section titles are quoted so each claim can be checked. "Implied" marks a reading that the record supports but does not state.

## 1. Creation: what was built, in what order

The archive's sections are in handoff order (newest at the top), but the older block (from "Champion's Tower (done)" down to "Next step") reads chronologically and the sections cross-reference each other, so the order below is reliable to within a phase.

**Phase 0, the claude.ai era (before Nov 3; no transcript).** The game began as a canvas prototype ("game logic ported from the original canvas", in "Architecture"), then a world port: 5 generated maps (town + 4 zones), movement, barricades, NPC sight lines, encounters, an objective arrow. The first content was MECHANICS AND NUMBERS, not place or story: "Systems and key numbers" is a table (20 rolls/day, 40 sightings, keep costs, XP table, gates Lv1/3/6/9, trainer stats, simulated win rates). Critters were GENERATED BY RULE from a seed (`pixel.js`: "critter geometry → pixel sprites"); 48 species; the three later secret critters are the only hand-designed ones. Maps were generated from configs (`buildZone`, seeded scatter, "stamp-after-scatter"). Then an "Endgame phase" (the "Next step" section says "Endgame phase is complete (Tower, shinies, milestones/titles all live)"): Champion's Tower (20 floors tuned with `towersim.py`), shinies (1 in 200, measured over 200k seeds), titles, music (7 synthesized loops), battle effects "designed without a reference video", play stats, aggro music. Then an expansion phase: cities (Mossbrook by hand, the other three "use `buildCity(CITY_CFG[id])` (same street plan as Mossbrook)"), the four routes ("built with `buildZone`"), city items, side quests, interiors (three furniture layouts x per-city colours), trainer sight rules, weekly Tower twist, follower + fishing, "Guidance without arrow". The phase ended with "Next step": "Dan declined dailies and the rival for now... Waiting on Dan's phone playtest + stats. Candidate features: weekly Tower twist, daily challenge battle, Tower leaderboard". Story at this point was a set of NPC lines and a conquest spine (seals, "Champion"); there was no stated theme.

**Phase 1, Claude Code, Nov 3 to Nov 5 (before the first preserved commit).** GitHub Pages + Supabase saves (Nov 3). Then, in archive order: weather + one ability per type, "Landmarks: reusable environment blocks" (a prop registry placed by nearest legal spot), ambient scenery critters, Dex polish, "Flavor lines + new people", accessibility, menu + map transitions, "Evolution look" (procedural limbs, "Not drawn by hand"). Then the first STORY content: "Adventure part 1: the Sealed Arch" (four clues from the people by the landmarks; a hall with eight statues and "an empty ninth seat... the setup for the secret critter"), parts 2 and 3 (the rite, guards, Keeper Orlen, Veilcrown), "The Cold Ascent + the Lamp Tower" (Dan's idea), "Developer tools + Wickling", "The Cinder Descent + the Forge Below + Slagback" (built on "whatever you think is best": the Descent is "a vertical flip of `buildAscent()`"). Then the combat rewrite: "Real-time battles (done as an opt-in beta)", "Action mode prototype" phases 1 to 6, steering, VFX, catch orbs, "Settings cleanup", "'Critter follows you' removed". Then structure: "No guidance + branching routes", "Mystery by hinting, never telling", "All four city routes are branching networks", the painted world map, the "Feedback round" (jitter + a level-design review), the bot walk test, save compatibility, housekeeping. Only at the END of this phase does the "Meaning" section appear (see §3), followed by "The help that leaves" (words-only revamp) and the signature places.

**Phase 2, Nov 5 to Nov 7 (53 preserved commits).** Nov 5: wind in five commits, Wren's gym trial pilot, test runner speed. Nov 6 (38 commits): test process review and mutation checks; Wren's gym REDONE ("tending is staying"); the other three gym trials; houses as statements; architecture per region; rooms for the Shrine, Arena and Watch; ambient drift; the Long Watch reworded as nights; flavor lines as observations; clues as things you look at; `telltest`; the rule refined ("hint and play"); "The player's road" (14 stations); "Linking the road" (title screen, Lamp Tower rebuilt, the ending built, the daily loop's lamp); night music; the opening rebuilt; eight facings; type verbs. Nov 7: attacks retuned, name tags removed, the Arena as its own zone (Roll Shrine removed, turn-based battle screen removed), map-edit hook, the cartridge shelf.

**Generated by rule vs designed by hand.** Rule: critters, their evolutions and facings, every map's terrain, the cities (one plan reused), the routes (one recipe), the interiors (three layouts), music loops from note strings and their night versions ("built in `music.js` from `NIGHT_LEAD`"), VFX frames, props placed by "nearest legal spot". Hand: the three dungeons' layouts, the secret critters, the twelve city rooms (Nov 6, "a room built by hand"), the gym trials, the signature objects, the NPC lines. The record shows the hand-built share rising sharply only after the Meaning section: the "Feedback round" admits that before it "every side map was the same recipe, an open meadow with one wandering trail", and that "A real fix for 'spirit' needs more distinct places (ruins, a gorge, a lake shore), not just terrain noise."

## 2. Iteration: what triggered revisions

Three triggers appear, in roughly this ratio: Dan playing on his phone (most of the rebuilds), tests and bots (bugs and layout faults), and principles (the Meaning rule, "hint, never tell").

**Dan playing.** Every section title that carries a Dan quote is a revision triggered by play: the arrow was "too guided" ("Guidance without arrow"), then guidance was removed entirely ("No guidance + branching routes"); evolutions "were just bigger blobs"; the keep popup "hid what you were getting"; the trainer encounter "gets stuck"; "every entity started shaking uncontrollably"; "objects shake a lot when moving"; "the trees go really fast then slow down"; "the animations move too much"; "the world doesn't feel lively enough"; "the game lacks mystery"; the switches "clutter the interface"; "most players lose interest after a minute". Each of these is play feedback on feel, and in every case the builder had already reported the item as "done".

**Tests and bots.** `walktest.py` found that "the Sealed Arch gallery's only way north crossed a glowing-moss patch" and that touching the crown selected the Keeper; `lamptest`'s state-graph search was itself wrong at first; `duelsim` "Finding 1: untuned, the bot won every dungeon trainer 100%"; the rhythm test found the wind pose jumping ("LESSON: my earlier checks of the wind were stills... nothing looked at motion OVER TIME"); the mutation passes found snapshots missing a number drift and a wording reversion. The sims corrected first guesses repeatedly: Veilcrown's first stats were "WEAKER than an ordinary good party"; Drizzle and Veil "were halved"; the Tower twists' "First tries... were far off"; the fed heavy blow was "strictly worse than tapping".

**Principles.** After the Meaning section, revisions came from the rule itself: the seals and Tower reworded; tips withdrawn; the clue-givers rewritten because they "deliver clues as exposition"; `telltest` written to lint the whole world; every house rebuilt "as a statement"; the Tower's floors turned into nights because "the Tower was a floor counter".

**What was rebuilt (not just patched).** Combat four times (turn-based → real-time beta → action prototype → permanent action mode with verbs; the turn-based screen deleted Nov 7). Guidance three times (arrow → words → nothing). The routes twice (single zone-recipe maps → branching networks → reviewed for "spirit"). Wren's gym twice in 5 hours (Nov 5 22:20 "tend smallest first"; Nov 6 02:32 "tending is staying"). The Lamp Tower twice (lever parity puzzle, with a state-graph proof of solvability → lamps you stay beside, "nothing closes behind you"). The Tower's framing twice (words → nights kept by lasting). The keep flow twice (chips → orbs and pouch). The opening once, late (Nov 6). The wind in five passes in one evening.

**The recurring "Not verified" note.** Almost every section ends with one, and they say the same thing in different words: "how it FEELS (tempo, whether 1 px sway reads as wind on a phone)"; "Not heard by Dan; the tunes may need taste tweaks"; "whether the withdrawal reads as intended to a player, or just as odd lines"; "that players notice them, what they look like in the game (checked only on a blank-canvas art sheet)"; "whether a player notices the pattern at all"; "that the trial is fun or clear"; "Every tempo is one constant... waiting on his feel". The core handoff ends with "Feel, tempo, video and real phones cannot be judged from here; say so in the report." Implied lesson: the process could verify structure, reachability and numbers continuously, but FEEL only when Dan played, and Dan's play lagged far behind the build (the Meaning section records "Dan's first three days (about 2.5 h, level 6, two seals, no Tower, no secrets)" against a world of three dungeons, two climbs, a Tower and four routes).

## 3. Meaning: when it entered, how it spread, what resisted

**The exact point.** The Meaning section first exists in the first preserved commit (Nov 5 18:48) and is placed above everything else. In the archive's own order it comes directly after the "Feedback round" whose title carries Dan's "you follow my instructions but do not grasp the spirit, review it and fix what you find", and after the level-design review's admission that terrain noise was not a fix for spirit. Before that, the nearest things to a theme were Dan's "the game lacks mystery" and "the sense of mystery is based on hinting but never telling" (both after the dungeons were built). The CLAUDE.md rule records the trigger sentence: "humans function in metaphors and symbolics". So the sequence is: ~40 content sections → a "spirit" complaint → a theme READ OUT OF the existing text ("Dan confirmed this reading of the existing text (he did not write the theme himself...)"; "Evidence already in the text: eight statues and an empty ninth seat... Keeper Orlen... Old Marta 'Four fires and one lamp'"). The theme was found, not set: the ninth seat had been built as "the setup for the secret critter", the keepers as boss gatekeepers, the route props as mystery dressing. The Meaning section then lists "Known contradictions to resolve": the conquest spine, the meaningless Tower, weather trivia, cities that "mean only their element", clue-givers as plot devices, and the deep layer "reachable only through side content".

**Propagation, in order.** (1) Words first, with mechanics frozen: "The help that leaves" is explicitly "Words and tips only: no price, reward, rule or balance changed"; the Tower revamp "Words only; the battles, floors, rewards and twists are unchanged". (2) Objects: one signature thing per route, zone, city and climb (lantern road, camps, the returned things at dawn, the wave of lamps, sprouts that grow with seals, a flame in ice, a hearth, a moored skiff, a growth post, a kettle, a workbench, a harbour lamp). (3) Ambiance: wind per region with the cold at zero ("wind symbolises change, but not in the cold, where it symbolises stillness"); snow falling straight; drift and smoke "where a fire is kept"; flags that hang still in the cold. (4) Mechanics: the four gym trials ("each the act that carries its meaning"), the Lamp Tower as lamps kept, the Tower as nights lasted, the rare grass that "stirs for the one who stays", the eight type verbs each from "the place's own metaphor", the heavy blow "fed, not rolled", guard as a stance. (5) Architecture: five regional house styles, twelve hand-built rooms, Home and the Lab as statements, name tags removed in favour of marks. (6) Sound: an audit of tempo and wave per region, night arrangements, a silent title "so the first sound of the game is one you caused". (7) Structure: "The player's road", fourteen stations each with meaning, "Introduced by", and a status (TOLD / HINT ONLY / PLAY / NO INTRO / INTRODUCED + PLAYED). (8) The rule sharpened on Nov 6 from "hint, never tell" to "hint and play the metaphor, don't tell it": "Play means the mechanic itself carries the meaning... A hint on its own, with nothing to play afterwards, is the weak form."

**What resisted.** Things that were pure counters or pure text: the Tower (unresolved in the first draft, "Candidate: the long watch"), the weather lines (still OPEN in the core handoff as "type trivia"), the clue-givers (still OPEN as exposition). The procedurally uniform routes resisted until hand features were added. The early scaffolding (Mom, tips, shop) resisted in a different way: it had to be withdrawn without looking like a bug ("withdrawn support must be felt, not look like a bug"). The Reduce-motion player is an unresolved case ("Which meaning the Reduce-motion player should keep").

**What had to be rebuilt because it carried nothing.** The road table records it: Highland Pass and the arch were "HINT ONLY for the road; the arch is PLAY, NO INTRO"; Moss Forest "PLAY, weakly introduced". The Lamp Tower's lever puzzle carried no meaning and was replaced; the Tower's floor counter likewise; the Roll Shrine was given a meaning room on Nov 6 ("gave without being asked") and removed on Nov 7 with the Arena rework; the "Keep" chips, the objective arrow, the settings switches, the name tags, the turn-based screen all went. Wren's first trial ("tend the smallest first") was a puzzle themed to the type; the second ("a seedling grows only while you stay beside it") is a verb the player performs, and the second is what the rest of the game then reused ("Introduced by: the Moss stir (same verb, stay)").

**Against Dan's conclusion (a), "meaning was defined too late... that is why the project could not go forward".** The record supports the first half plainly: meaning entered after the whole content base, as a reading of what was there, and the first revamp step had to leave mechanics untouched. It supports the waste (see §5). It does NOT record a stall after meaning entered; the opposite: the 48 hours after the Meaning section are the densest in the history, and the road table shows thirteen of fourteen stations reaching INTRODUCED + PLAYED. What the record shows stalling is earlier and different: the "Next step" of the claude.ai era ("Waiting on Dan's phone playtest + stats"; candidate features listed as a menu) and the combat work, which went through three systems before "we have the definitive version". Implied: the project's forward motion before meaning was feature accretion steered by play complaints; what meaning changed was that every later change could be judged ("Every review of existing content starts with 'what does this mean?'"), and the "could not go forward" is better described as "could not be judged" (implied, not stated). One more caveat the record itself gives: nobody had played the revamp ("he has not yet reported a play-through of the revamp"), so whether the meaning-first content works is as unverified as everything before it.

**For and against Dan's conclusion (b), "the ending should have been defined first and the road worked out backwards".** For: the ending was the LAST thing to acquire meaning. The Tower was built in the "Endgame phase" as "a conquest ladder" tuned by `towersim`; the first Meaning draft lists "Champion's Peak and the Tower: unresolved"; the ending station ("the lamp lit, the ninth seat taken, the keepers rest... you no longer need someone to keep it for you") was built only on Nov 6 ("Play (built Nov 2026)"). The Meaning section itself is ending-anchored: "Lighting the lamp is the answer to the question the game asks from the first minute." And the rule's "hint first, as the introduction" is structurally a backwards dependency: the road's "Introduced by" column can only be filled once the later station is known, which is why several stations were "NO INTRO" (the arch's seat had nothing before it until the two chairs at Home were added). The core handoff states the generalised version: "The player's road below is the structure the experience is built on: define it before building." Against, or qualifying: the road was in fact written in gate order, start to end, not backwards; and the first thing that failed with real players was the OPENING ("most players lose interest after a minute"), fixed last (Nov 6: the spawn "mid-plaza facing the locked south gate, the only open zone 22 tiles west and off screen... Hint, then nothing to do"). Dan's "refinement" of the theme (independence, not inheritance) also came after the ending content existed, which shows the ending's meaning itself moved once stated. Implied conclusion the record supports: both ends needed defining before the middle; the ending gives the metaphor its answer, the opening gives the player a reason to walk toward it, and the middle (the stations) is where hints and plays are placed relative to both.

## 5. Dead ends: removed, reversed or wasted

- **Objective arrow and quest marker** → goal pill with words → removed entirely ("Guidance without arrow", then "No guidance + branching routes"). Lesson stated: "trust the players". The guidetest's arrow check later turned out to prove nothing ("read 128 on every build, so its earlier 0 was not evidence").
- **Real-time battles (beta)**: a full system with its own sim, deleted entirely in "Settings cleanup". Then **the turn-based battle screen** itself, the game's original combat, deleted Nov 7 with `fxtest`/`fxperf`. Lesson implied: three combat systems coexisted behind switches until Dan called one "the definitive version"; the switches themselves were then named clutter.
- **Keep chips** → catch orbs and pouch. Lesson stated: the popup "hid what you were getting".
- **Settings switches** (`rtb`, `arpg`, `ctrlcrit`, `keepall`, `follow`) all removed; "Critter follows you" "makes no sense anymore".
- **The Roll Shrine**: given a hand-built room with offerings and candles on Nov 6 04:00; removed Nov 7 02:19. The in-arena room likewise built Nov 6 and gone Nov 7 when the Arena became a zone. Lesson implied: dressing a place with meaning does not settle whether the place should exist.
- **Place name tags** removed ("a place says what it is by how it is built").
- **Conquest wording** ("Champion", "beat me and you hold every seal") replaced throughout.
- **Three cities on one street plan** → varied layouts later; **four routes on the zone recipe** → networks → still "the same recipe" until the level-design review. Lesson stated: "not just terrain noise".
- **Wren's first trial** (smallest first) replaced within hours by "staying". **The Lamp Tower's lever parity puzzle**, with a state-graph proof of solvability, replaced by lamps you stay beside. Lesson implied: a correct puzzle is not a meaningful one; the proof was spent on the wrong design.
- **The Tower as floors** → nights. **The heavy blow's 75% miss** removed ("in a real-time game a dropped input is not a mechanic"); the first fed version "strictly worse than tapping".
- **Declined features**: dailies, the rival, daily challenge battle, Tower leaderboard (the "Next step" menu).
- **Dead code and tooling**: `logic.js`, `critter.html`, `helmet.html`, `build.py`, `reassemble.py` left over; `lbal*`, `towersimab`, the source tarball removed; `peaktest`/`towertest` compared against an old build that did not exist ("proves nothing").
- **Wrong first measurements**: the lamp graph search, the wind stills, the arrow pixel check, snapshots that masked a number drift. Lesson stated: "a snapshot proves 'still prints what it printed'; anything that MATTERS as a number or a sentence needs a `check()`."

## 6. Tools that served the process (one line each)

- **Critter generator** (`pixel.js`, seeds → sprites; evolutions, eight facings "from the same parts"): let 48 species, two further forms and eight facings exist without drawing them, so art never gated a build.
- **Map builders** (`buildZone`, `buildCity`, `buildWild`/`NETS`, `addProp`/`addSpot`/`addTrail`, `landmarks.js`, `ambient.js`): a whole world from specs, placed by rule; later the same hooks let one hand-placed meaning object be dropped into each generated place.
- **Synthesized music and VFX** (`music.js` note strings and night versions derived from day chords; `vfx.js` generated frames; `vfxsheet.py`, `mapsheet.py` contact sheets): sound and effects per place with no assets, auditable by numbers (bpm, peak levels) if not by ear.
- **Balance sims** (`towersim`, `lbal*`, `itemsim`, `dgsim`, `secretsim`, `rtsim`, `actsim`, `duelsim` driving the real code): tuned every number before anyone played, and caught designs that were trivially winnable or strictly worse.
- **Bots that play** (`walktest`, `openingtest` timing the first keep and the second minute, `actsim2`, `duelsim`'s mash and verb bots): proved reachability and pace in real time, and found layout faults; they cannot value dodging or feel.
- **Real-time tests** (`windrhythmtest`, `verbtest`, `facingtest`, the gym tests "timed over real time"): after the wind stall, anything animated is checked frame-against-clock, because stills hid rhythm bugs.
- **Lints for meaning** (`telltest`, the `wildtest` wording guard): made "don't tell" enforceable across every NPC line, sign and object text.
- **Snapshots, `exact.txt`, `chk`, mutation passes**: a cheap regression net, then a measured one ("break it on purpose and see what goes red").
- **Dev tools** (teleport, ignore gates, adventure shortcuts): let anyone reach the deep content that normal play (2.5 h, level 6) never did.
- **Map editor + `mapedits.json` + GitHub workflow**: hand edits to the generated maps without touching the generator.
- **Play stats log**: built to compare real play against the sims; the record never shows the stats arriving.

## Summary against the two conclusions

Meaning entered late and was read out of content already built; the record supports that half of (a) and shows the cost in §5. It does not show the project stopping afterwards; it shows it unable to be judged before, and unverified by play after. On (b), the record shows the ending was the last place to get meaning and that the rule's "hint first" makes every earlier station depend on knowing the later one, which is a backwards structure; but it also shows the opening failing first in real hands. The handoff's own stated conclusion is the general form: define the road before building.
