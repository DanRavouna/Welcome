# 08. Creative director: the whole, the reviews, the shape, the list

Read in full: the brief, `docs/process.md`, the seven reports (01 to 07). Claims that the shape below rests on were checked in `src/world-rev/` (`greatLampUse`, `lampState`, `homeLitNow`, `objState`, `musicTrack`, `world.js` 1218 to 1248, `data.js` gates, `flavor.js` Mom, `itemUsable`, the save of the player's position). Nothing else was touched.

## 1. The whole

What the game means as it stands, element by element:

- **The road** says: a land of keepers holds dark things until a strong enough trainer beats them and collects what they kept. Level gate, sign, trainers, boss, tap, +30, title. Four trials and three tower lamps genuinely play tending; the rest is conquest with tending words on it.
- **The words** say two things: 370 observations that carry the sentence without naming it, and 120 tells placed exactly where the meaning is densest (the leaders after their trials, the three keepers, Coral, Marta, the tablet, "Touch the glass").
- **The places** say "a hub, four zones, four towns, four gyms", with the sentence laid on as props a "least open tile" rule hides in corners. Three layouts say it outright: the title, Home's door, the Arch hall.
- **The mechanics** say: keeping is killing then buying; a seal is a knockout count; growth and parting are prices; the verbs that are the metaphors lose to mashing; help that should leave is a dead button; after the ending, keeping is one Examine press with no consequence.
- **The art** says the right thing by day (the still cold, Cinderport's fed fires, a dark tower with two torches "for whoever comes") and the wrong thing at night about the one object that matters: Home's window lights itself (`world.js` 1244, no `homeLit` check). The HUD says "app".
- **The sound** says "a place of this temperature" and "tap registered". No act is heard; the four halls play the village theme; the great lamp plays the jackpot jingle; keepers fight to the villain cue.
- **The pacing** says the opening is right (Wren's hall at two minutes with no empty screen) and then the day is a fuse (twenty rolls in ninety seconds of bot play), the gates count species, and the ending sits at level 11 with two dungeons and twenty nights after it.

**The contradictions a player would feel**, in the order they would feel them:

1. **Nothing is ever missing.** The window is lit from the first second (and at night by a draw loop, whatever the state), the road lights itself ahead, a knocked-out party gets up where it fell, help never leaves except in words ("Words only; no price, reward or rule changes", the code's own comment). The sentence is about the moment you no longer need it kept for you; the player never once feels it kept, because it is never once withdrawn. Independence that was never dependence is nothing.
2. **The ending inverts the sentence.** At the top the player is told to touch the glass, fights a Warden to reach it, is paid 30 essence with the roll jingle and handed a secret critter; then "Its light reaches the whole land": every lighthouse beams by itself, day and night, the arch glows, and at Home both windows glow every night by themselves. After the act that is supposed to mean "you keep it now", the land is kept for you more than before. The one thing the code does right (`own:` posts, `homeLit`) is invisible: a saved game resumes where it was (`critter-roll:world` stores the position), so a player who continues from Frosthold never sees the dark window at all.
3. **The loop's verb contradicts the trials' verb.** The player tends four times and kills two hundred times. `kill()` bursts the critter into particles, drops an orb, the pouch sells it back. Whatever the trials say, the loop says it louder.
4. **The last act is in the middle.** Ascent at 11, Forge at 13, the Watch after four seals, and the Forge ends with the lamp's finale copied line for line. Two identical finales make neither last, and the real last thing most players do is the Arena ladder.
5. **The day is spent before it is lived.** Twenty rolls, ten keeps, then eighteen minutes of "Needs 1 rolls". A day that "holds what it holds" is felt as a day that mostly refuses.

**Noise** (true, not felt as contradiction): the twenty route boxes sharing one recipe (walked one at a time); the Descent mirroring the Ascent (days apart); the title silhouette; Glacia's flickering braziers; the crabs on the Coast; fog bars; colliding names; the HUD's radii; the titles' two registers; cups on every table. Fix where cheap, never spend a week.

**What holds and must not be lost:** the title and the door; the four trial mechanics; the cold's stillness in every element at once (the one place where mechanic, wind, snow, music and words already agree, the model for the rest); the Arch hall; the architecture per region; the night arrangements; the observations; Mom's "The window was dark when I came down. I left it."

## 2. The reviews

**Where they are right, and agree.** All seven converge on the ending being asked for (road F1, words F1, mechanics M3, pacing 4 and 6), priced (road F2, words F6, mechanics M3, sound F3, art F7) and in the middle (road F14, pacing 4): the ending is the bug. Two found the night-window draw bug independently and pixel-verified it (places 1, art F1; road F9 is the adjacent design fault). Mechanics W4 and pacing 16 agree on `STILL_S`; mechanics W3 and road F31 on the Tend button; road F29 and mechanics P3 on the `???` rows; road F23, words F10 and pacing 10 on the partner card. That much is settled.

**The findings only one report could make, and that matter most:**
- Mechanics M1 (Keep is kill-and-loot). Nobody else looked at the loop; everyone else audited stations. It is the deepest finding in the review.
- Mechanics M5 (the five battle items cannot be used in action mode: `itemUsable` lives in `game.js` and nothing in `action.js` calls it; verified). The guard rail "withdrawn support must not look like a bug" is broken literally, on day one, by accident.
- Pacing 9 (Wren's hall opens at two minutes, the fire is kept days later: `lv: 11, needs: 'ivy'`; verified). The first station's act and its fire are separated by the thing the sentence is not about.
- Sound F1 (the four halls play `town`; verified), and the note that `musictest`'s snapshot pinned the bug as a pass: a warning about snapshot discipline worth more than the fix.
- Places 3 and 13 (`addSpot` puts the signature thing in a corner 25 to 31 tiles off the road): the hints exist and are not met. Pacing 1 (the fuse) and 2 (58% of days hand out a quest that cannot progress): measured, not argued.

**Where they overreach.**
- Road F12 (travel only on roads lit end to end) punishes the player for finishing. Refused.
- Road F11, places 11, pacing 13 want the real clock replaced or bent. The real clock is this game's decision (night is your night) and the Watch's premise. They are right that day players miss five hints; the fix is day states for the hinted things (the skiff dry by day, the ahead-post dim by day, the glass visible), not a new clock.
- Mechanics M9 (evolution by days led) is a new progression system with guessed numbers: the claim is right, the mechanism is Dan's to design.
- Mechanics M10 proposes a balance programme from a bot that never dodges. The separate cooldown is one line; the rest waits for a thumb.
- Mechanics W11 (a knocked-out party walks home after the beacon) is punishment; "being looked after is good" holds after the end too. Refused.
- Road F25 (an act on every route) and F35 (a daily log for the furnace) multiply chores and dilute the verb. One daily act; one route act at most.
- Words proposes 230 replacements; by its own count about thirty carry meaning (F1 to F6, F14, F16). The inventory is useful; the judgement is in the thirty.
- Art F9 (the HUD) is Dan's frame to replace, by the process. Written up, not shipped.
- Places 5, 12, 19, 20, 22 are generator work: the generated world is a placeholder, and finishing it now is building before the station is filled.

**Where two disagree.**
- The climax: road F2 wants nothing given, no toast, no jingle, music dropping; words F6 wants the essence granted silently and the beam dialog kept; mechanics M3 keeps the title. Decided below: nothing given, one line, the title appears the next morning.
- The Warden: road F4 says she is not fought; mechanics M4 keeps the fight and makes the lamp a stay. Both are right about different things: the proof is the three lamps, so she does not stand between; and the great lamp is kept by staying, longer than any lamp below it.
- The lit window's consequence: road F13 (a critter waits by the door, Mom out), mechanics M8 (your posts are lit only on a day the window is; the window refills the day's lamp; Wickling the first evening), sound F13 (`town@dark`). Taken: M8's posts and Wickling, sound's dark arrangement, road's label; refused: the refill (it makes the window a battery) and Mom out (her chair is the seat counted for you; it stays).
- Gates: pacing 3 and mechanics W8 give compatible seal counts; the numbers below merge them, and pacing 19's warning (the Pass is skippable) is answered by opening the town's north gate only after Glacia, so the first road to Frosthold is through the Pass and the Arch.
- Fern: places 9 moves her to the path; pacing 23 would not; words F9 and road F22 disagree on her line. Taken: a body, one observation, "Go on, then."

**Where they all missed the same thing.**
1. **Nobody asked who the keeper is.** All seven treat the window as an object. The window is Mom's. The keeper who visibly stops is not Maren, Hale or Orlen (strangers met once); it is the person who lit the window every night of the game. Only road F8 and F9 brush it. The withdrawal is hers, once, in one line; the guard rail (home and its people intact) is met by her still being there, sitting, not lighting.
2. **Nobody asked where the day begins.** The game resumes where you were. After the lamp, the dark window is seen only by a player who happens to walk home; every review proposes consequences for the lit window and none noticed most players would never see it dark. The daily act needs a daily return.
3. **Nobody noticed the Watch contradicts station 13.** The lobby's lamps "do not go out" (handoff, station 12) and the Watch opens at four seals, before the lamp: a place kept for you by nobody, still burning after the night you became the keeper. The Watch's meaning is only coherent after the lamp, as the keeper's nights: people come to your door now.
4. **Nobody weighed scope.** Four fires, three dungeons, twenty nights and a five-day ladder, for one sentence. The reviewers inventory; nobody cut. The process says scope is what the road needs, and the Forge (a twin) and the Arena (play with no stated meaning) are not needed by it.
5. **Nobody weighed the risk of the fix.** The road and mechanics reports propose a post-beacon game that is darker, slower and more locked. Done carelessly, the ending makes the daily game worse, and "withdrawn support must be felt, not look like a bug" cuts both ways. One daily act at the door, six seconds, the land answering: that is all the player should pay for being the keeper.

## 3. The shape

**The last act: the great lamp, kept by staying.** Nothing names it before the climb: no title in the list, no `???` row, no "beacon" on the card, sign or Journal (the place is "The Cold Ascent"), no tablet, no Warden's "Touch the glass", no action label. Maren is not between the player and the lamp; she sits (her LETGO line is already right). The great lamp is a `chamberlamp` with a 6 s stay, lit only from the three kept below, no button. Nothing is given: no essence, no toast, no jingle, no critter. One line at most ("The glass is warm."), the beam, silence, then the lit arrangement, and the walk down with the posts lighting as you pass. The title "The lamp was lit" appears the next morning, past tense. Wickling is the hidden station the lamp earns: asleep on the sill the first morning you light the window.

Is it then unasked? Inside the tower, yes: four dark rooms, three stays already made, one dark glass left. The climb itself is wanted by two things built below: the dark window at Home when the Ascent opens, and the Ascent being the only road that opens then. Whether a dark window is felt as loss or as a bug is Dan's to judge on the phone, and that is why Mom's one line exists.

## 5. What to leave alone

- The opening's geometry: the lit window above the door, grass two tiles away, the critter waiting, the one lit gate fourteen tiles off. `openingtest` guards it; keep the test's numbers.
