## 1. What the words mean now

The words of Critter Roll are two games written on top of each other. The newer layer (the interiors, the route cairns, the returned things, the Arena, Mom's withdrawing lines, the keepers' resting lines) is one voice: short, declarative, the second sentence an observation the player can project onto ("Two cups; one has no ring in it.", "The window was dark when I came down. I left it."). It carries the sentence without naming it, and it is most of the 370 observations. The older layer is a monster-collecting template: signs that list types and name the gym, trainers who announce battles and rewards, shopkeepers selling the same goods as the last shop, tutorial townsfolk, quest-givers who "make it worth your while". It says nothing about the sentence; it says "RPG". Where the two layers meet (the leaders' win lines, Coral's fourth seal, the three keepers, the beacon), the new layer stops hinting and tells: the metaphor is explained in the moment it was played, and the last act is requested. The words introduce the metaphor well, play it well, and then explain it, which the rule forbids.

## 3. What could not be judged from here

- Whether a line is read at all: the typewriter runs at 2 characters per 28 ms (`typeLine`), and a long sign is a toast of 3.8 s. On a phone, in motion, many of the longer tells may never be read, which cuts both ways: the harm of the telling is smaller than it looks on paper, and so is the good of the observations. Only a play-through with Dan says which lines land.
- Tone against music: I cannot hear whether the trainers' exclamation marks clash with the place themes in practice.
- Whether the hints are noticed before their acts (Mae's "My mother used to stand in it and wait", the two cups, the empty chair) is what the words are for, and the one thing a bot cannot report.
