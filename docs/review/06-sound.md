## For the directors

1. The music is a map of places, not of acts: every track is chosen by map id and hour; no act (a lamp kept, a fire fed, the seat taken, the great lamp lit, a night kept) changes anything heard. The score never plays the metaphor; at best it rhymes with the place.
2. The four trial halls (`gym-wren`, `gym-glacia`, `gym-blaze`, `gym-coral`) play Sproutvale's cheerful theme: no alias exists for `gym-*`, so `musicTrack()` falls back to `'town'`. Coral's dark hall sounds like the village square. Worst bug, two-line fix.
3. Ten sound effects serve the whole game, and the acts of keeping share two: `tap` (880 Hz, 40 ms) is kneeling by the bed, a stage of growth, ice forming, a log taken, the fire fed, a Coral lamp touched, every door; `claim` ([784, 1047]) is the title touch, a lamp kept, the seat answering, the window lit, a critter kept, a kill, a swap, a quest reward.
4. Coral's call is silent: the lamps light in order without a note; the answer is the same click for all five. The trial built on "answering across the dark" cannot be played by ear.
5. The great lamp, the forge and a secret critter joining all play `mythic`, the Mythic-roll jingle, and the Tower's track does not change after the beacon. Nothing is lit in sound.
6. The Lamp Tower, the darkest place, has the brightest dungeon arrangement (G major, kick/snare/hat at 88 bpm, more drums than the climb outside). The Watch's nights drop the lobby's held `watch` for the generic 150 bpm `battle`; a kept night ends on the fight-won jingle.
7. Silence is used once (the title) and any tap on the dark part of the title starts the town theme under the still-visible title (verified). Every crossing elsewhere is a 0.5 s crossfade.
8. Dusk sounds like noon: night arrangements start at 21:00, the lantern road lights from 18:00.
9. Right and to keep: the cold is still (frost/frosthold triangle, 80/84 bpm, two hats a bar, `frost@night` rests on 42 of 64 steps), Ember drives (126, every step), the Arch is quiet (70, one hat a bar), the night versions are a real idea (same chords, fewer, lower, held, 0.62 volume), and the first sound is one the player causes.
10. Nearly every change below is data: aliases, new note lists in `sfx()`, arrangement variants picked by flags already saved (`cl:L1..L3`, `lampLit`, `forgeLit`, `rubble`, `homeLit`), and `MUSIC.play(null)` for silence, which `apply()` already supports.

## 1. What this element means now

Honestly: the score says "you are in a place of this temperature" and the effects say "the game registered your tap". Each outdoor place has a tune whose tempo and wave fit the zone's draft meaning reasonably (slow triangle for Moss and the cold, square and drums for Ember, a thin 0.125 pulse for the water), and every outdoor tune has a night form that is quieter, lower and sparser, the one place the music carries an idea. But the road's stations are acts (stay, feed, answer, keep, last the night, light it yourself), and no act is heard. The lamp takes in silence and announces itself with the shop's two notes; the fire dies without a sound; the seat answers with the quest chime; the great lamp plays the roll jingle. Halls are generic (the village theme) and dungeons static (the Tower before and after the beacon are one track). Fights are scored as fights, keepers as enemies (`boss`, C minor), a night kept as a fight won. Sound is today the element that most often "says nothing", which the rule calls a bug of meaning.

## 3. What could not be judged from here

Nothing was heard. The offline render gives samples; I judged structure only: tempo, wave, register, density, chord motion, what is silent and when things change. Whether a track is pleasant, whether 84 bpm feels still or merely slow, whether square 0.25 reads "bright" or "thin" on a phone speaker, whether the 880 Hz click is crisp or irritating, whether the night arrangements are recognised as the day's tune (`NIGHT_LEAD` is a different melody over the same chords), and whether any note list above sounds like its name (a flame taking, a fire guttering, ice): only ears can say; the WAV `musictest` writes to `/mnt/user-data/outputs/critter-roll-music-preview.wav` is how to put them in front of Dan. The timing constants proposed (0.6 s ticks, 1.6 s of silence at the lamp, one bar at dawn) are to be tuned by listening; whether Coral's pitches are learnable in three rounds needs a player; real phone output (mono speaker, autoplay rules, Bluetooth latency between the touch and the `light` sound) is unverified.
