## Meaning (read this first; Dan, Nov 2026)
Humans read in metaphor. The game is the greater meaning; the quest is inside it; each level is inside the quest. Dan confirmed this reading of the existing text (he did not write the theme himself, so refine it with him rather than treat it as fixed).
- **The game (theme): home is something someone keeps lit for you, and the journey ends when you become the one who keeps it. Dan's refinement: becoming the keeper means you no longer need someone to keep it for you.** The ending is independence, not inheritance: lighting the lamp is the moment you realise you can carry your own light. The keepers (Maren, Hale, Orlen, the hermit) held it for you until you could, so their "prove you can carry it" is letting go, not gatekeeping. The early scaffolding (Mom, the Lab, the gifted partner, Pip's tips, Mira's potions) is the being-looked-after that the game should gradually and visibly withdraw. Guard rails: being looked after is good, not weakness; carrying your own light is not being alone (home and its people stay intact at the end); withdrawn support must be felt, not look like a bug. The ninth seat (the one left out) now supports this and no longer carries it. You start looked after (Mom, the Lab, a gifted partner), cross a land full of keepers who tend dormant things for a successor, and end by lighting the lamp and taking the ninth seat, which is the one the world left out. Evidence already in the text: eight statues and an empty ninth seat with an unclaimed crown; Keeper Orlen "Eight were enough for the world, they said. The ninth was kept here, asleep"; Veilcrown (Dusk, Last Light) and Wickling (Guiding Light); Warden Maren, Forgemaster Hale and Keeper Orlen each keep something dark or cold until someone proves they can carry it; Old Marta "Four fires and one lamp, and the whole country knew where home was"; Joss: the lighthouse lamp points inland at the arch, "whoever built it wanted the door found"; and the unexplained route props (new rope, second cup, warm lantern, new stone, new ribbon, polished runners, wet skiff, empty chair) all say someone tends this for someone not yet arrived.
- **The quest:** the four seals are four fires you are trusted to tend (not trophies); the three side places are the three things that must be kept (the seat: who is counted; the light: the way home; the fire: the work); the Peak is the long watch. Lighting the lamp is the answer to the question the game asks from the first minute.
- **Known contradictions to resolve in the revamp:** the spine is written as conquest ("Champion", "beat me and you hold every seal"); the Tower is a floor counter with no meaning; weather lines are type trivia; the four cities and zones mean only their element; the four clue-givers are plot devices; the deep layer (keepers, ninth seat) is reachable only through side content, and Dan's first three days (about 2.5 h, level 6, two seals, no Tower, no secrets) were all on the conquest spine.
- **DRAFT meaning of each place (refine with Dan):**
  - Sproutvale: where you are kept. The lit window; the Shrine is the first thing that gives without being asked.
  - Moss Forest / Mossbrook: being tended while you grow (Elder Wren: "grow together"); first seal = first fire.
  - Frost Peaks / Frosthold: what is kept by being kept cold; waiting preserved.
  - Ember Fields / Cinderport: the fire that has to be fed; work; the forge below is where it began.
  - Tide Shore / Coralbay: what the sea gives back; return; the last fire, where the road home begins.
  - The routes: the roads between the fires, kept walkable by people you mostly never meet; every route holds one sign of someone tending.
  - The Sealed Arch / Lamp Tower / Forge Below: the seat, the light, the fire.
  - Champion's Peak and the Tower: unresolved (a conquest ladder today). Candidate: the long watch, where the floors are nights kept, not rivals beaten.
  - Emphasis question settled by Dan (see theme): independence, you no longer need it kept for you.
- Revamp order proposed: (1) this section and the CLAUDE.md rule (done); (2) re-word the spine and the seals from conquest to tending without changing mechanics; (3) give every route, zone and city one meaning-bearing hand-built place instead of terrain noise; (4) decide the Tower; (5) put the deep layer on the main path in a way you can feel without being told.

## Project

- Live: https://claude.ai/artifact/W9Ta3mH9hfZQixkzJkpfyD (republish to this URL; omit `capabilities` to keep them; they are `{db:{}, downloads:true, user:{}}`).

## Architecture (single HTML, assembled from pieces in `world/`)
- `runtime.js` TPL: renders the screen markup (`sc-if`, `sc-for`, `dc-import`, `{{holes}}`) with DOM morphing; hole attributes renamed `data-h-*` at compile.
- `pixel.js` PIX: critter geometry → pixel sprites (`big` 0.25 scale, `icon` 0.075 shown 2x, `silhouette`), cached by seed:stage:mode.
- `pxworld.js` PXW: 16px pixel tiles per theme, buildings, player/NPC frames (`frames(pal)`).
- `world.js` WORLD: 5 maps (town 44x34 + 4 zones 34x28), movement, exits, locked-trail barricades, NPC sight lines, encounters, objective arrow, day/night tint, camera snapped to device pixels (no jitter).
- `cloud.js` CLOUD: cloud save to `data/users/{uid}/save`, lease (`acquire`, 4s) + re-check before every write, conflict dialog, local-first.
- `app.js`: `Base` → `Component` (game logic ported from the original canvas) → `Game` (overrides: make with levels, battleAction, party, trainers, encounters) → `APP` (HUD, menus, dialogue, people, map, backup, shop, party, bag, intro, hints, objective).
- `screen.html`: screen template (roll/dex/battle/trainer screens). Shell HTML holds HUD, overlays, CSS.
- Rebuild: `python3 build2.py` (JS order now runtime, pixel, pxworld, world, cloud, music, app) (self-contained: `world/shell.html` holds HUD/CSS/overlays with `/*@@JS@@*/` and `<!--@@SCREEN@@-->` placeholders; JS order runtime, pixel, pxworld, world, cloud, app). Output: /mnt/user-data/outputs/critter-roll-world.html. Buildings are drawn in `pxworld.js` `place()` (world.js `drawPlace` is dead code).

## Tests: how the process works now (Nov 2026)
- Setup once: `bash src/test-setup.sh`. Run: `bash src/run-tests.sh @smoke` after EVERY change (about 30 s); the tests of the feature you touched (`bash src/run-tests.sh name.py`); the whole suite once before pushing a batch (no argument, 71 tests, about 4.5 minutes with 6 side by side; the suite is mostly fixed sleeps, about 480 s of `wait_for_timeout` in 1100 s of test time, so the CPU stays half idle even at 6 jobs; `JOBS=3` is the quiet setting for diagnosing a flaky test, and the real-time wind tests held up under load). Do not add the groups after a full run, they are already in it.
- The runner prints one line per test and a final `SUMMARY: N tests | F failed | C changed since the snapshot`; it exits non-zero unless both are 0.
- **Snapshots** (`src/snap/<test>.txt`, `src/snapcmp.py`): most tests only print numbers, so each has a snapshot of the lines a clean run prints every time (built from three runs; lines that differ between runs are left out). A later run that no longer prints one of them is reported CHANGED with the missing lines. The date, the real-clock weather words and the rotating daily city quest are normalised for every test; numbers are masked as `#` in the comparison for every test except the 34 listed in `src/snap/exact.txt` (steady across many runs), so a True/False flip, changed words or a missing line still fail but timing noise (live duels, animation, positions, pixels) does not; a numeric drift in a masked test is NOT caught, so put a real expectation in a `check()` when a number matters. A test that is not clean is repeated once before it is reported; if the repeat is clean it is shown as FLAKY (never hidden). After an INTENDED change, read the diff, then accept it with `bash src/snap-build.sh name` (one test, three fresh runs) or `bash src/snap-build.sh` (all, about 25 minutes). Never rebuild to make a red run green without reading the diff.
- **Known stale checks** (print a constant False; kept visible, labelled STALE, not hidden): `shinytest` sprite comparison and `towertest` "replay blocked" (it simulates the retired turn-based flow; the live Tower is covered by `acttest5`). The first audit of the snapshots also found and fixed: `evotest` fingerprint broken by the new `seedLabel` field, `jittertest` disturbed by wind (now run with Reduce motion), `leadtest` (the Moss sprouts bed sat in the zone's only big clearing: `addSpot` now picks the LEAST open tile near the hint so zones keep their open ground for fights), `acttest` hook artifact.
- **New tests** use `from chk import check, done` (`src/chk.py`): `check('what must be true', cond, detail)` prints ok / FAIL and `done()` exits non-zero if any failed. Test anything animated over real time (`windrhythmtest.py` logs the pose against the clock): stills hide rhythm bugs.
- `p17`, `menutest`, `transitiontest`, `devtest`, `compattest` run without the seed hook; everything else with it (`run-tests.sh` handles it). `wplay` uses real randomness: repeat a flaky result before calling it a regression. `supatest.py` (real cloud) is separate, see `CLAUDE.md`.
- Where the time goes and what would cut it further (measured Nov 2026): the sum of test times is about 1100 s, 480 s of it explicit sleeps (704 `wait_for_timeout` calls; 152 of them are the 1.0 to 1.5 s wait after `goto` for APP to exist). The next real saving is replacing those post-load sleeps with `wait_for_function("typeof APP!=='undefined' && APP.game")` (about 180 s of sum, done mechanically across 84 files) and shortening the 300 to 900 ms sleeps after actions into waits on a condition; the two real-time wind tests (100 s together) are long by design. Sub-agents do not speed tests up: the limit is this machine's 4 cores and Chromium, and parallelism is a shell matter (`xargs -P`), already done.
- Feel, tempo, video and real phones cannot be judged from here; say so in the report.
