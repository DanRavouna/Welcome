## Meaning (read this first; Dan, Nov 2026)
Humans read in metaphor. The game is the greater meaning; the quest is inside it; each level is inside the quest. Dan confirmed this reading of the existing text (he did not write the theme himself, so refine it with him rather than treat it as fixed).
- **The game (theme): home is something someone keeps lit for you, and the journey ends when you become the one who keeps it. Dan's refinement: becoming the keeper means you no longer need someone to keep it for you.** The ending is independence, not inheritance: lighting the lamp is the moment you realise you can carry your own light. The keepers (Maren, Hale, Orlen, the hermit) held it for you until you could, so their "prove you can carry it" is letting go, not gatekeeping. The early scaffolding (Mom, the Lab, the gifted partner, Pip's tips, Mira's potions) is the being-looked-after that the game should gradually and visibly withdraw. Guard rails: being looked after is good, not weakness; carrying your own light is not being alone (home and its people stay intact at the end); withdrawn support must be felt, not look like a bug. The ninth seat (the one left out) now supports this and no longer carries it. You start looked after (Mom, the Lab, a gifted partner), cross a land full of keepers who tend dormant things for a successor, and end by lighting the lamp and taking the ninth seat, which is the one the world left out. Evidence already in the text: eight statues and an empty ninth seat with an unclaimed crown; Keeper Orlen "Eight were enough for the world, they said. The ninth was kept here, asleep"; Veilcrown (Dusk, Last Light) and Wickling (Guiding Light); Warden Maren, Forgemaster Hale and Keeper Orlen each keep something dark or cold until someone proves they can carry it; Old Marta "Four fires and one lamp, and the whole country knew where home was"; Joss: the lighthouse lamp points inland at the arch, "whoever built it wanted the door found"; and the unexplained route props (new rope, second cup, warm lantern, new stone, new ribbon, polished runners, wet skiff, empty chair) all say someone tends this for someone not yet arrived.
- **The rule, refined by Dan (Nov 2026): hint and play the metaphor, don't tell it.** Play means the mechanic itself carries the meaning, across the whole game including the daily loop; every element of a place (mechanic, layout, what is seen and hidden, colours, sound, words, pacing) conveys the same intent; the hint is the introduction, a first smaller encounter with the concept placed anywhere earlier on the road, so the play feels already met; nothing explains. The player's road below is the structure the experience is built on: define it before building.
- **The quest:** the four seals are four fires you are trusted to tend (not trophies); the three side places are the three things that must be kept (the seat: who is counted; the light: the way home; the fire: the work); the Peak is the long watch. Lighting the lamp is the answer to the question the game asks from the first minute.
- **The contradictions found in the first reading, and where they stand (Nov 2026):** the spine was written as conquest -> DONE (seals handed over as fires, Vigil Peak / The Long Watch / Fire-keeper / Watchkeeper); the Tower was a floor counter -> DONE as the long watch (lobby room, keeper lines; floors and rewards unchanged); the deep layer reachable only through side content -> DONE (keepers rest, lighthouse beams, Home's window, the arch glows); cities and zones meant only their element -> DONE (one signature place each, four gym trials, every house a statement, architecture per region); weather lines are type trivia -> OPEN (`flavor.js` still says "Volt critters love this weather"); the four clue-givers are plot devices -> OPEN (Edda, Dell, Joss, Neri still deliver clues as exposition; Joss and Brandt got one letting-go line each). Dan's first three days (2.5 h, level 6, two seals) were all on the conquest spine; he has not yet reported a play-through of the revamp.
- **DRAFT meaning of each place (refine with Dan):**
  - Sproutvale: where you are kept. The lit window; the Shrine is the first thing that gives without being asked.
  - Moss Forest / Mossbrook: being tended while you grow (Elder Wren: "grow together"); first seal = first fire.
  - Frost Peaks / Frosthold: what is kept by being kept cold; waiting preserved.
  - Ember Fields / Cinderport: the fire that has to be fed; work; the forge below is where it began.
  - Tide Shore / Coralbay: what the sea gives back; return; the last fire, where the road home begins.
  - The routes: the roads between the fires, kept walkable by people you mostly never meet; every route holds one sign of someone tending.
  - The Sealed Arch / Lamp Tower / Forge Below: the seat, the light, the fire.
  - Vigil Peak and the Long Watch: the long watch, where the floors are nights kept, not rivals beaten (built: names, keeper lines, a lobby room; every floor is a night, the visitors have plain names, the weekly rules are weathers of the nights).
  - Emphasis question settled by Dan (see theme): independence, you no longer need it kept for you.

What remains is listed under "To do" at the end.

## Linking the road (Nov 2026, Dan: "link everything into a meaningful and cohesive experience")
One verb runs the whole road: keep a light. Each station below is its meaning sentence, written before building (the rule).
- **Title screen**: the introduction to everything: one lit window in the dark, kept by someone before you did anything; the game begins when you touch that light.
- **Home, the two chairs**: a seat has always been counted for you; the first, quiet meeting of "who is counted", long before the ninth seat.
- **Tide Shore, the returned things**: what the sea gives back is met on the shore itself, before Coral asks you to answer a call.
- **The roads after the beacon**: the lamps that lit themselves ahead of you no longer do; from the night you light the great lamp, a post lights when you pass it and stays lit, saved: the roads are kept by you now.
- **Home after the beacon**: the window is dark until you light it, each day; being the keeper is a small daily act, not a line.
- **The Lamp Tower**: the way home is lit by you: the chambers are dark, each gate opens when you have stayed beside that chamber's lamp until it takes, and the great lamp lights only from the lamps you kept below it.
- **The daily loop**: a day holds twenty rolls and forty sightings, and nothing carries over: the rolls chip is the day's lamp and its flame goes down as the day is spent; what you keep is what comes home with you, and the critter you keep sleeps in the basket at the Lab; letting go costs nothing; the Shrine gives without being asked.
- **Night versions of the place themes**: at night the world is quieter, and each place is still itself in the dark: a melody written for the night over the day's chords (fewer notes, lower, held), slower on a softer wave with no drums and one bass note a bar: the hint, every night, of the quiet before someone comes to a door (the Watch).
- **The Watch's lobby**: a track of its own, slow and held, few notes that do not resolve: a lamp that does not go out, waiting.
- **The opening** (Dan, Nov 2026: "most players lose interest after a minute, the initial direction is difficult to feel"): you begin at the door of the house whose window you just touched, with the road in front of you; the first thing to keep is within a few steps (a small patch of tall grass beside the path); the one open gate, west, has a lamp lit on it by day, the road someone keeps for you, and the three others are shut; Fern points at what you can see. Nothing explains.
- **The Watch's nights**: a night is kept by lasting it, not by beating whoever comes: the visitor comes in at the lobby door, the night runs its length (about half a minute, longer on the long nights) and ends at dawn with the lamp still lit, or earlier if they all go down; the lobby itself is where it happens, by the lamps that do not go out.

## Architecture (single HTML, assembled from pieces in `world/`)
- `runtime.js` TPL: renders the screen markup (`sc-if`, `sc-for`, `dc-import`, `{{holes}}`) with DOM morphing; hole attributes renamed `data-h-*` at compile.
- `pixel.js` PIX: critter geometry → pixel sprites (`big` 0.25 scale, `icon` 0.075 shown 2x, `silhouette`), cached by seed:stage:mode.
- `pxworld.js` PXW: 16px pixel tiles per theme, buildings, player/NPC frames (`frames(pal)`).
- `world.js` WORLD: 5 maps (town 44x34 + 4 zones 34x28), movement, exits, locked-trail barricades, NPC sight lines, encounters, objective arrow, day/night tint, camera snapped to device pixels (no jitter).
- `cloud.js` CLOUD: cloud save to `data/users/{uid}/save`, lease (`acquire`, 4s) + re-check before every write, conflict dialog, local-first.
- `app.js`: `Base` → `Component` (game logic ported from the original canvas) → `Game` (overrides: make with levels, battleAction, party, trainers, encounters) → `APP` (HUD, menus, dialogue, people, map, backup, shop, party, bag, intro, hints, objective).
- `screen.html`: screen template (roll/dex/battle/trainer screens). Shell HTML holds HUD, overlays, CSS.
- Rebuild: `python3 build2.py` (JS order now runtime, pixel, pxworld, world, cloud, music, app) (self-contained: `world/shell.html` holds HUD/CSS/overlays with `/*@@JS@@*/` and `<!--@@SCREEN@@-->` placeholders; JS order runtime, pixel, pxworld, world, cloud, app). Output: /mnt/user-data/outputs/critter-roll-world.html. Buildings are drawn in `pxworld.js` `place()` (world.js `drawPlace` is dead code).

## Tests: how the process works now (Nov 2026)
- Setup once: `bash src/test-setup.sh`. Run: `bash src/run-tests.sh @smoke` after EVERY change (about 30 s); the tests of the feature you touched (`bash src/run-tests.sh name.py`); the whole suite once before pushing a batch (no argument, 78 tests, about 4.5 minutes with 6 side by side; the suite is mostly fixed sleeps, about 480 s of `wait_for_timeout` in 1100 s of test time, so the CPU stays half idle even at 6 jobs; `JOBS=3` is the quiet setting for diagnosing a flaky test, and the real-time wind tests held up under load). Do not add the groups after a full run, they are already in it.
- The runner prints one line per test and a final `SUMMARY: N tests | F failed | C changed since the snapshot`; it exits non-zero unless both are 0.
- **Snapshots** (`src/snap/<test>.txt`, `src/snapcmp.py`): most tests only print numbers, so each has a snapshot of the lines a clean run prints every time (built from three runs; lines that differ between runs are left out). A later run that no longer prints one of them is reported CHANGED with the missing lines. The date, the real-clock weather words and the rotating daily city quest are normalised for every test; numbers are masked as `#` in the comparison for every test except the 33 listed in `src/snap/exact.txt` (steady across many runs), so a True/False flip, changed words or a missing line still fail but timing noise (live duels, animation, positions, pixels) does not; a numeric drift in a masked test is NOT caught, so put a real expectation in a `check()` when a number matters. A test that is not clean is repeated once before it is reported; if the repeat is clean it is shown as FLAKY (never hidden). After an INTENDED change, read the diff, then accept it with `bash src/snap-build.sh name` (one test, three fresh runs) or `bash src/snap-build.sh` (all, about 25 minutes). Never rebuild to make a red run green without reading the diff.
- **What the suite catches, measured by mutation (Nov 2026, Dan: "are these tests still reliable?")**: five deliberate bugs were injected one at a time. Caught: the cold gaining wind (`windtest`), the gym vines never opening (`gymtest`), a changed NPC line (`scaffoldtest`). Missed, then closed with `balancetest.py` (explicit `check()` expectations, in `@smoke`): a pure number drift (DUEL_HP 2.65 -> 4: masked numbers hide it everywhere else) and the seal wording reverting to conquest (nothing asserted on the leaders' lines). Rule of thumb from this: a snapshot proves "still prints what it printed"; anything that MATTERS as a number or a sentence needs a `check()` with the expected value written down. Re-run the mutation idea whenever a new system lands: break it on purpose and see what goes red.
- **Known stale checks** (print a constant False; kept visible, labelled STALE, not hidden): `shinytest` sprite comparison and `towertest` "replay blocked" (it simulates the retired turn-based flow; the live Tower is covered by `acttest5`).
